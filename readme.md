# Waveform Design: Linear Frequency Modulation Delay - Doppler Domain Modulation: A New Paradigm for Integrated Perception and Communication in Autonomous Vehicles [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract: Autonomous driving is reshaping human transportation, with millimeter-wave radar playing a crucial role in realizing the connectivity revolution between vehicles and everything else (V2X). Although chirp signals are widely used in millimeter-wave radar systems due to their strong perception capabilities, existing systems lack integrated communication functions, which could potentially limit further development of autonomous driving.
Subsequently, researchers proposed two delay-Doppler domain modulation schemes based on linear chirp, enabling each pair of millimeter-wave radar transceivers to simultaneously perceive and communicate within their respective time-frequency resource blocks. These two modulation schemes are based on different multi-input multi-output (MIMO) radar approaches: one based on Time Division Multiple Access (TDM) offers higher communication rates, while the other based on Doppler Division Multiplexing (DDM) is suitable for lower signal-to-noise ratio ranges.
The effectiveness of the proposed DDM-based scheme was verified through simulations, and the researchers pointed out several challenges and issues that need to be addressed to promote the integration of perception and communication (ISAC) technology in V2X and achieve better autonomous driving.
Introduction (Overview): For a long time, linear chirp waveforms have been widely applied in radar systems. Linear chirp signals are easy to generate, have constant magnitude, and are convenient for receiver processing. With the advantages of simplicity in implementation and low hardware costs, they have been widely favored [1]. In recent years, millimeter-wave radar systems based on linear chirp have demonstrated outstanding perception performance, especially in the field of autonomous driving - they form the three core sensors of autonomous vehicles together with LiDAR and cameras.
Based on linear chirp millimeter-wave radar systems can overcome the limitations of laser radar and visual systems under adverse weather conditions, while also offering superior speed estimation capabilities [2]. The perception results obtained by autonomous driving vehicles using linear chirp radar can provide environmental information, support rapid formation driving of vehicles, and ensure seamless and secure V2X communication.
The successful application of linear frequency modulation (LFM) signals in the radar field has inspired researchers to explore their potential for simultaneous perception and data transmission. Since LFM signals were originally designed for radar, related research often focuses on perception-centric scenarios with moderate communication rates, contrasting sharply with orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM)-centric waveforms [4]-[6].
Although OFDM offers high spectral efficiency, in the millimeter wave band, high Doppler shifts can lead to inter-carrier interference (ICI), making it difficult to achieve efficient speed estimation and reliable communication. Research has explored delay-Doppler domain signal processing frameworks such as Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) modulation to enhance Doppler resilience [7], and there have been attempts to combine the advantages of OFDM and LFM by proposing new waveforms like Orthogonal Linear Frequency Modulation Division Multiplexing (OCDM) and Amplitude Modulation Division Multiplexing (AFDm) [8]. However, these waveforms, despite offering high spectral efficiency and certain perceptual capabilities, require more complex hardware and higher computational complexity at the expense of low hardware cost, high perceptual capability, and low communication rate, which does not match the autonomous driving industry's demand for low hardware costs, high perceptual capability, and low communication rates.
Key parameters of LFM signals include: 1) Carrier frequency, 2) Bandwidth, 3) Pulse Repetition Interval (PRI), 4) Phase, 5) Amplitude. Therefore, some studies have proposed that by changing these parameters without altering the signal's linear frequency modulation spread spectrum (CSS) characteristics, data can be carried, for example, through amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), and phase modulation (PM) schemes [9]. To maintain the CSS characteristics of LFM and ensure perceptual capability, the pulse repetition interval (PRI) as the duration of a single LFM signal is used as the time unit for data transmission.
Therefore, compared to the communication-centric scheme that modulates data at the sampling point level, the linear frequency modulation (LFM) based scheme has inherent limitations in terms of communication rate. Depending on specific requirements, the pulse repetition interval (PRI) for LFM can vary from a few microseconds to several dozen microseconds, corresponding to communication rates ranging from several thousand kilobits per second to several hundred kilobits per second.
Conclusion: Linear frequency modulation signals currently play a key role in ensuring the safety of autonomous driving. This paper improves the system by integrating communication functions into the linear frequency modulation signals in millimeter wave radar systems, promoting the development of autonomous driving and vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication technologies.
Firstly, the study proposes a Chirp-DMA scheme, enabling multi-pair millimeter wave radar transceivers to achieve uninterrupted integration of perception and communication (ISAC). Secondly, two different multi-input multi-output (MIMO) radar architectures (Time Division Multiple Access (TDM) architecture and Doppler Divided Multiple Access (DDM) architecture) are designed based on linear frequency modulation, allowing each pair of millimeter wave radar transceivers to synchronize their perception and communication.
Through simulations of the perception and communication capabilities based on the Doppler Divided Multiple Access (DDM) scheme, the effectiveness of the proposed delay-Doppler domain modulation scheme based on linear frequency modulation is validated. Finally, this paper also points out open issues in the field and potential future research directions.
